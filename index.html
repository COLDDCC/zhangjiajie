Start here

Zhangjiajie is three places, not one

Almost every wasted day here comes from the same mistake: assuming the Avatar mountains, Tianmen Mountain and the glass bridge are the same attraction. They are three separate ticketed areas in three different districts, each needing its own ticket on its own day.

Zone 01 · Wulingyuan

The pillar forest

Zhangjiajie National Forest Park — Yuanjiajie, Tianzi Mountain, Yangjiajie, Golden Whip Stream. One ticket, valid four days, and it needs two of them. Base yourself in Wulingyuan town.

Zone 02 · The city

Tianmen Mountain

A single mountain rising straight out of Zhangjiajie city, with the cave, the cliff walkways and the hairpin road. Half a day. The cable car boards in town.

Zone 03 · Cili County

The Grand Canyon

Home to the 430-metre glass bridge, about 15 km northeast of the forest park but a good 60 km from Tianmen Mountain. Half a day, easily paired with Huanglong Cave.

Glass bridge in detail →
The most common booking error Tianmen Mountain has short glass walkways bolted onto a cliff. The Zhangjiajie Grand Canyon Glass Bridge is a free-standing bridge across a canyon, an hour away by road. Photos of the two get mixed up constantly, and people book the wrong ticket. If you want the bridge, book the Grand Canyon.

The pillars are the reason you came. They are a third of the trip.

The list

The best things to do in Zhangjiajie

Ranked by how much of a trip they justify. The figure beside each entry is its vertical dimension — because in Zhangjiajie, height is the whole point.

1,080 mPillar summit

01Yuanjiajie and the Avatar Hallelujah Mountain

This is the view everyone came for: a plateau of freestanding quartz-sandstone columns, some over 300 metres tall, drifting in and out of cloud. The pillar renamed after the floating mountains in Avatar stands at the main platform, and the crowd around it is dense — walk ten minutes further along the loop to the First Bridge Under Heaven and it thins out sharply.

Most people arrive via the Bailong Elevator, a glass lift bolted to a 326-metre cliff face. It is genuinely startling for about forty seconds. The queue can run past an hour in summer; the eco-bus and the cable car from Tianzi Mountain reach the same plateau without it.

  • Time neededHalf a day minimum
  • TicketCovered by the park pass
  • Extra costBailong Elevator charged separately
  • GoBefore 09:00 or after 15:30
1,518 mSummit

02Tianmen Mountain and Heaven's Gate

A completely different landscape from the pillar forest: one huge massif with a 131-metre hole punched clean through it. You ride up, walk the cliff-edge plank paths around the summit, then climb — or take the tunnel escalators down to — the 999 steps beneath the cave. The road below, Tongtian Avenue, folds back on itself 99 times and is worth watching from above.

The glass sections of the cliff walkway cost a few yuan for shoe covers and have a stone-floor bypass at every point, so vertigo is not a reason to skip the mountain.

  • Time needed4–6 hours
  • Ticket≈¥288, cableway included
  • EntryTimed slots — book ahead
  • BaseZhangjiajie city, not Wulingyuan
2026 operations The Tianmen Cave express cableway restarted on 20 March 2026 and routes A, B and C are running again, but sections of the main cableway have been in and out of renovation since late 2025, with an eco-bus substituting on part of the climb. Full route breakdown here — and reconfirm the week you travel, because third-party guides are slow to update this.
~300 mAbove canyon floor

03Walk the Grand Canyon Glass Bridge

430 metres long, six metres wide, glass the whole way, with a few hundred metres of air underneath. It is the most photographed thing in the region after the Avatar pillars, and the crossing takes about fifteen minutes — so treat it as one item on a canyon half-day rather than a destination in itself.

The canyon below is the better half of the ticket: a descending path past waterfalls, a boat ride out across Shenquan Lake, and a very long escalator back up. Do the bridge first, then walk down.

  • Time neededHalf a day
  • Ticket≈¥178 canyon + bridge
  • From Wulingyuan≈30–40 minutes
  • NoteCloses in high wind
7.5 kmHorizontal

04Hike Golden Whip Stream end to end

The one flat walk in a vertical place, and the most underrated few hours in the park. A paved streamside path runs between the pillars rather than above them, so you spend the whole hike looking up at 300-metre walls with macaques on the railings. No climbing, no cable car, no ticket beyond the park pass.

Walk it from Shuiraosimen towards the Forest Park gate to move against the tour groups, and keep food out of sight — the monkeys are practised thieves.

  • Time needed2.5–3 hours
  • DifficultyFlat, paved, all ages
  • Best lightLate afternoon
  • TicketCovered by the park pass
1,262 mPeak

05Catch sunrise from Tianzi Mountain

The highest of the park's viewpoints and the reliable place for a cloud sea — the pillars stand in white and the ridgelines float. It only works when the humidity cooperates, which in practice means an early morning after rain, most often between April and June.

Staying overnight inside the park at Tianzi Mountain village puts you on the platform before the first cable car arrives. That is the whole trick.

  • Time needed2 hours
  • Best oddsMorning after rainfall
  • AccessCable car or eco-bus
  • BringA layer — cold at dawn
1,092 mRidge

06Huangshizhai, the widest view in the park

A local saying holds that skipping Huangshizhai means you never really came to Zhangjiajie. The ridge gives pillar fields in three directions rather than one framed platform, and because it sits near the main gate, day-trippers routinely run out of time before reaching it.

The cable car takes six minutes. The stairway takes about ninety and is a real climb.

  • Time needed2–3 hours
  • AccessCable car or ~3,900 steps
  • CrowdsLighter than Yuanjiajie
  • TicketCovered by the park pass
585 mLake elevation

07Boat across Baofeng Lake

A small reservoir sitting improbably high between the peaks, circled by a thirty-minute boat loop. Tujia singers call to each other from wooden platforms along the shore as you pass — staged, unapologetically, and still one of the more charming half-hours in the region. Good for an afternoon when your legs have had enough of stairs.

  • Time needed1.5–2 hours
  • TicketSeparate from the park pass
  • Pairs withAn easy Wulingyuan day
140 mChamber height

08Huanglong Cave when the weather turns

One of China's largest show caves: kilometres of chambers on several levels, an underground river you cross by boat, and a stalagmite so treasured it is reportedly insured. The lighting is emphatically coloured. It is also completely weatherproof, which makes it the standard answer to a rained-out day in Wulingyuan.

  • Time needed2 hours
  • TicketSeparate from the park pass
  • Best forRain days, hot afternoons
60 mWaterfall drop

09Day trip to Furong Ancient Town

A Tujia stilt-house town built on top of a waterfall, about two and a half hours south. You can walk behind the falls on a ledge cut into the rock and look out through the water at the village. Late afternoon into dusk is the time to be there, once the lanterns come on and the day tours have left.

Fenghuang Ancient Town, further on, is the bigger and more famous version of the same idea — but it needs an overnight to be worth the drive.

  • Time neededFull day
  • Distance≈2.5 hours each way
  • Go forSunset and evening lights
EveningNo climbing

10An evening show, then Xibu Street

Two folk spectaculars run nightly: Charming Xiangxi in Wulingyuan, a theatre production of western Hunan legend and Tujia wedding custom, and Tianmen Fox Fairy, staged outdoors on the mountainside with the cliffs themselves as the set. Neither is subtle; both are a good use of an evening when your legs are finished.

Afterwards, Xibu Street in Wulingyuan is where the grilled skewers, rice tofu and sour-fish stalls are. Order the ciba — glutinous rice cakes, pounded fresh.

  • Time needed2 hours
  • BookingSame-day is usually fine
  • EatSour fish, ciba, cured pork
Fitting it together

How many days do you need?

Two days covers the headlines badly. Three covers them properly. Four leaves room for a cloud sea, if the weather offers one.

Two days

  1. Forest park — Bailong Elevator up, Yuanjiajie, then Tianzi Mountain
  2. Tianmen Mountain in the morning, evening train or flight out

Three days

  1. Forest park — Golden Whip Stream, then Yuanjiajie in the afternoon
  2. Tianzi Mountain at sunrise, Huangshizhai after lunch, a show in the evening
  3. Grand Canyon and the glass bridge, then Tianmen Mountain or out

Four days

  1. Golden Whip Stream and Yuanjiajie; sleep inside the park
  2. Tianzi Mountain at dawn, Yangjiajie, Huangshizhai
  3. Grand Canyon, glass bridge, Huanglong Cave
  4. Tianmen Mountain, or a full day at Furong Ancient Town

Practicalities

When to go, and how to get there

Best months. April to June, and September into early November. Spring brings the mist that makes the pillars look like the photographs; autumn brings the clearest air and the most comfortable walking. July and August are hot, wet and busy. Winter is quiet and occasionally spectacular under snow, but cliff paths close in ice. Month-by-month comparison →

Dates to avoid. The first week of October, and the Labour Day holiday at the start of May. Queues for the Bailong Elevator and the Tianmen cableway can run into hours, and beds double in price.

Arriving. High-speed rail from Changsha to Zhangjiajie West station takes two to three and a half hours, with more than fifty services a day; the fastest run under two hours. Zhangjiajie Hehua Airport handles domestic flights. Both sit near Zhangjiajie city, roughly forty minutes by road from Wulingyuan, the town at the forest park gate.

Where to sleep. Wulingyuan for the pillar forest — you will spend two days there. Zhangjiajie city only if your trip is short and Tianmen-focused. Inside the park, at Tianzi Mountain, if a sunrise cloud sea is the reason you came.

What to wear. Grippy shoes, a light waterproof, and a warm layer even in summer — summits run several degrees cooler than the valley, and cloud on the walkways is wet.

Questions

Zhangjiajie FAQ

Is Zhangjiajie really where Avatar was filmed?

Not filmed — referenced. The film's floating Hallelujah Mountains were digitally created, with the production team drawing on photographs of these sandstone pillars among other karst landscapes. In 2010 one of the columns at Yuanjiajie was officially renamed Avatar Hallelujah Mountain, and the association stuck.

Which is better, Tianmen Mountain or the National Forest Park?

The forest park, if you only have time for one. It is the landscape Zhangjiajie is famous for and rewards two full days. Tianmen Mountain is a single dramatic mountain with remarkable engineering, and it fits comfortably into a half day either side of your train.

Is the glass bridge safe, and what if I'm afraid of heights?

The glass is laminated, thick, and inspected regularly, with a strong safety record. Comfort is the real question rather than engineering. On Tianmen Mountain every glass section has a stone-floor path alongside it, so the mountain is fully doable without stepping on glass. The Grand Canyon bridge is glass for its full 430 metres — there is no alternative route across.

Can I visit Zhangjiajie without speaking Chinese?

Yes, with preparation. Signage in the scenic areas is bilingual and ticket gates are self-service, but taxi drivers, small restaurants and guesthouse staff generally speak no English. Have your accommodation written in Chinese, set up a translation app that works offline, and arrange transfers between scenic areas in advance. Many independent travellers hire a driver for the Grand Canyon day.

How far is the glass bridge from the Avatar mountains?

Around 15 kilometres as the road runs from the forest park, or thirty to forty minutes from Wulingyuan town by bus. It is a separate scenic area with its own ticket, and it is much further from Tianmen Mountain — closer to sixty kilometres.

Is one day in Zhangjiajie enough?

One day buys you the forest park's headline viewpoints — Bailong Elevator, Yuanjiajie, Tianzi Mountain — and nothing else. It works as a stop between Changsha and Fenghuang. It is not enough to add the glass bridge and Tianmen Mountain as well.
