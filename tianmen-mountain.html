Tianmen Mountain

A single massif rising straight out of the city, with a 131-metre hole punched through it, one of the world's longest cable cars strung up its flank, and a road below that folds back on itself 99 times. Half a day, if you pick the right route.

Check the cableway before you commit

Tianmen Mountain has been running with partial cableway service, and most English-language guides still describe a setup that has changed.

What we know as of July 2026 The Tianmen Cave express cableway — the shorter line between the mountain gate and the cave — was suspended for maintenance in late 2025 and restarted on 20 March 2026, restoring round-trip service on routes A, B and C. Separately, sections of the main cableway between the city and the summit have been under renovation since November 2025, with an eco-bus substituting on part of the climb. Neither situation is guaranteed to hold. Reconfirm on the scenic area's own booking channel in the week you travel.

The practical consequence is simple: do not build a trip around riding the full length of the cableway in one continuous journey. Build it around getting to the summit, which is always possible one way or another.

Choosing

Routes A, B and C

You do not wander around Tianmen Mountain. You buy into a fixed route, and the route determines the order you see things in and how long you queue.

AClassic

Cableway up from the city, cave last

Board the main cableway in the middle of town, ride up the mountain flank with the 99-bend road unspooling below, tour the summit loops, then descend through the mountain to the cave and finish at the gate. This is the route most people picture when they picture Tianmen Mountain, and the one that puts the best scenery at the start while you are fresh.

It is also the busiest. In peak season the cableway queue in town is the single longest wait on the mountain.

  • Best forFirst visit, good weather
  • Watch forMorning queues in town
  • CaveDescend the 999 steps
BReverse

Shuttle bus up, cableway down

The mirror image: a shuttle takes you up the hairpin road to the mountain gate, you climb or ride to the cave, escalators carry you to the summit, and you finish with the long cableway ride back down into the city. The bus up the 99 bends is a genuine experience of its own — and a rough one if you get motion sick.

Choose B when the town-side cableway queue is already an hour deep at opening, which it often is by mid-morning in summer.

  • Best forBeating the morning queue
  • Watch forTravel sickness on the bends
  • CaveClimb the 999 steps
CShort

Bus both ways, mountain only

The shuttle-and-escalator option that skips the long cableway entirely. Cheaper in time if not always in money, and the right answer on a day when the cableway is down, the weather has closed in, or you simply have a train to catch. You still get the cave, the summit and the skywalks — you just miss the ride.

  • Best forTight schedules, bad weather
  • You missThe long cableway ride
  • TimeRoughly 3–4 hours
On the mountain

What you actually do up there

  • The rideThe cableway. Around 7,455 metres end to end and close to half an hour, climbing over farmland, then terraces, then the switchbacks of Tongtian Avenue. Sit facing backwards for the better view of the road.
  • The loopWest and East cliff walks. Plank paths pinned to the rock face, circling the summit. The west line is the more dramatic; the east is quieter and passes Tianmen Temple.
  • The glassThree skywalk sections, including Coiling Dragon Cliff. A small charge for shoe covers at each. Every section has a stone-floor path alongside it, so you can walk the whole loop without stepping on glass.
  • The holeTianmen Cave. A natural arch 131 metres high, reached by a stairway of 999 steps or by a chain of escalators bored through the mountain. Standing under it is the point of the day.
  • The roadTongtian Avenue. Ninety-nine hairpins climbing to the cave. You see it from the cableway and drive it on the shuttle — there is no walking option.
Costs on top of the ticket The summit's small peak-forest cable car and the lower escalator section to the cave are charged separately, as are the shoe covers. None is expensive individually; together they add up to more than people budget for.
Practicalities

Timing, tickets and getting there

Where it is. The lower cableway station sits in central Zhangjiajie city, a short bus ride from Zhangjiajie West high-speed rail station. This is the one major attraction you do not base yourself in Wulingyuan for — from there it is about forty minutes each way.

Tickets. Around ¥288 for a one-day pass covering the scenic area and round-trip cableway. Entry is by timed slot, tickets are name-registered, and you will need your passport at the gate. Book a few days ahead in July, August and around Chinese public holidays; a day ahead is usually fine in spring and autumn.

How long. Four hours if you move; six if you walk both cliff lines and linger at the cave. It fits neatly into a morning before an afternoon train, which is exactly how most three-day itineraries use it.

What the weather does. Cloud sits on this mountain often, and a white-out at the summit is common — atmospheric on the plank paths, useless for the view. Ghost Valley plank road and parts of the cave route can close in ice or heavy snow. Month-by-month conditions →

In the evening. The Tianmen Fox Fairy show is staged outdoors on the lower slope, using the cliffs as its backdrop. It runs seasonally and cancels in bad weather.

Questions

Tianmen Mountain FAQ

Is Tianmen Mountain the same as the Zhangjiajie glass bridge?

No. Tianmen Mountain has short glass walkways attached to a cliff face. The Zhangjiajie Grand Canyon Glass Bridge is a 430-metre free-standing bridge in a different county, around sixty kilometres away, with its own ticket. Details on the bridge here.

Do I have to climb the 999 steps?

No. Escalators bored through the mountain connect the cave and the summit in both directions, and route A has you descending the steps rather than climbing them. If you do climb, allow twenty to forty minutes and take the handrail — the treads are steep and often wet.

Can I skip the glass skywalks?

Yes. Each glass section runs parallel to a solid stone path, and you can walk the full summit loop without setting foot on glass. The skywalks are also the only parts that carry a small extra charge, so skipping them saves a little money.

Is Tianmen Mountain worth it if I have already seen the forest park?

Yes, because it is a different kind of landscape rather than more of the same — one enormous mountain and a lot of ambitious engineering, instead of a field of pillars. If you only have time for one, choose the forest park; if you have three days, both fit.

How early should I arrive?

Be at the cableway station when it opens. The queue there grows faster than anywhere else on the mountain, and an hour's head start in the morning is worth more than any other planning decision you make that day.
