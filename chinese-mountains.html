Which mountain in China?

Compare the experience first. For Zhangjiajie, use the detailed route, entrance and accommodation guides.

The real question

Nobody visits "a Chinese mountain"

The lists you find ranked by height are useless for planning, because the tallest peaks in China are Himalayan and effectively unvisitable. The mountains that matter to a traveller sort along a different axis entirely: what the mountain asks of you.

Ask 01

Do you want to climb?

Taishan and Huashan are climbs with cable-car escapes. Huangshan and Zhangjiajie are lifts with walks attached. Emeishan can be either, over two days.

Ask 02

Temples or geology?

Taishan, Emeishan and Wudang are cultural landscapes where the buildings are the point. Huangshan and Zhangjiajie are rock and weather, with temples incidental.

Ask 03

How many days?

Huashan is a long day from Xi'an. Taishan is a day and a night. Huangshan needs two, Emeishan two to three, Zhangjiajie three or four.

One thing they share All six are heavily engineered: cable cars, stone stairways, paved paths, ticket gates, and crowds that peak on Chinese public holidays. None is wilderness. If you are looking for trailheads and solitude, you want Yunnan or Sichuan's high country, not this list.

Every classical landscape painting in China is arguing with a mountain.

The six

What each one is actually like

The figure beside each is its summit elevation, on one shared scale. It is the least interesting thing about any of them, which is rather the point.

3,099 mGolden Summit

SichuanEmeishan

The highest of the six and the only one where altitude is a real consideration. A Buddhist pilgrimage mountain rising out of the Sichuan basin, with monasteries strung up the slope where you can stay overnight, and a summit that spends much of the year above a cloud layer.

Two ways to do it: the pilgrim's version over two or three days walking between temples, or the cable-car version that gets you to the Golden Summit and back in one long day. The macaques along the paths are aggressive and organised — they are not the charming ones.

  • AskTemples, altitude, two days
  • BaseChengdu, ~2 hours
  • SleepIn a monastery, if you can
  • NoteCold at the summit year-round
2,154 mSouth Peak

ShaanxiHuashan

The vertical one. Five peaks of pale granite with near-sheer faces, connected by stairways cut into the rock and, on one notorious stretch, by wooden planks bolted to a cliff with a harness clipped above them. That plank walk is an optional side attraction with its own queue, and it is genuinely exposed.

Two cable cars do most of the elevation, which turns Huashan into a long day trip from Xi'an — the natural pairing with the Terracotta Army. Doing it properly on foot, including the old night climb to catch sunrise from the East Peak, is a different and much harder trip.

  • AskExposure, drama, one long day
  • BaseXi'an, ~1.5 hours by rail
  • Pairs withTerracotta Army
  • NoteThe plank walk is extra and queued
1,864 mLotus Peak

AnhuiHuangshan

The one that trained the Chinese eye for what a mountain should look like: granite spires, pines growing sideways out of rock, and cloud pouring between the ridges. Almost every classical landscape painting is arguing with this mountain. If you have space for exactly one, and you want the definitive version, it is this.

It needs two days and a night on top, because the sea of cloud happens at dawn and the last cable car down leaves in the afternoon. Summit hotels are expensive, basic and booked out well ahead. Below the mountain, the Huizhou villages of Hongcun and Xidi are worth the extra day.

  • AskThe classic view, two days
  • BaseHuangshan City / Tunxi
  • SleepOn the summit, booked early
  • Pairs withHongcun and Xidi villages
1,612 mTianzhu Peak

HubeiWudang

The Taoist one, and the least visited by foreign travellers of the six. A complex of temples and palaces spread across a forested range, built under Ming imperial patronage, with the Golden Hall at the summit. It is also the home of Wudang martial arts, and schools here take foreign students for stays of weeks or months.

As pure scenery it is the weakest on this list. As atmosphere — mist, cypress, morning practice in a courtyard — it is the strongest. Come here if the reason you are interested in Chinese mountains is Chinese, not mountains.

  • AskAtmosphere over views
  • BaseShiyan / Wudangshan town
  • UnusualMartial arts schools take students
  • CrowdsLightest of the six
1,545 mJade Emperor Peak

ShandongTaishan

Not the most beautiful and not the highest, but the most important — the mountain emperors climbed to report to heaven, continuously for two thousand years. The route is a stone staircase of some seven thousand steps lined with inscriptions carved into the rock by everyone from Tang poets to modern officials. You are walking through a text.

The traditional way is to start in the evening, climb through the night, and reach the summit for sunrise. It is a rite of passage for Chinese visitors and correspondingly crowded on summer weekends. A cable car covers the upper section for anyone who would rather sleep.

  • AskHistory, stairs, a night climb
  • BaseTai'an, ~2 hours from Beijing
  • The climb≈7,000 steps
  • NoteCable car skips the upper half
1,262 mTianzi Mountain

HunanZhangjiajie

The odd one out, and the one most foreign visitors have already seen without knowing its name. Not a peak but a field of more than three thousand quartz-sandstone pillars, standing free of each other, that fed the design of the floating mountains in Avatar. You look across it and down into it rather than up at it.

It is also the most engineered place on this list — a 326-metre glass elevator up a cliff, cable cars in four directions, and a separate scenic area an hour away with a 430-metre glass bridge across a canyon. That suits some travellers exactly and puts others off entirely. Three days minimum.

  • AskSpectacle, three to four days
  • BaseWulingyuan town
  • AccessRail from Changsha, ~2–3 hrs
  • NoteThree separate ticketed areas

The most confused booking in China

Two different sheets of glass

Tianmen Mountain has short glass panels bolted to a cliff. The Grand Canyon glass bridge is a 430-metre free-standing span sixty kilometres away. Photographs of the two circulate interchangeably, and people book the wrong ticket for the wrong district.

Two of these combine easily with things you are probably already doing: Huashan with Xi'an and the Terracotta Army, Emeishan with Chengdu and the pandas. Huangshan and Zhangjiajie are destinations in their own right and need to be planned around, not slotted in.

Applies everywhere

Five things true of all of them

The lifts stop before the light does. Cable cars close in the late afternoon on every mountain here, often well before sunset. Any plan involving evening light on a summit requires sleeping up there.

The entrance ticket rarely includes the ride. Cable cars, elevators and shuttle buses are usually charged on top of the gate price, and they add up to a substantial share of the day's cost.

Reservation arrangements vary. Check the reservation rules and transport timetable for the mountain and travel date you choose.

Two weeks a year are unmanageable. The National Day holiday in the first week of October and the Labour Day break in early May fill every one of these mountains past comfort and double the price of a bed.

Summits are much colder than you expect. Reckon on five to eight degrees below the valley on the lower mountains, more on Emeishan, plus wind. Bring the layer even in July.

Questions

Choosing a mountain: FAQ

What is the most famous mountain in China?

Huangshan, for scenery — it is the mountain classical landscape painting keeps returning to, and the one most Chinese travellers name first. Taishan is more culturally significant but less spectacular, and Zhangjiajie is more recognisable to foreign visitors thanks to Avatar, even though many could not name it.

Which Chinese mountain is best for a first visit?

Huangshan if you want the archetype and have two days. Huashan if you are already in Xi'an and have one. Zhangjiajie if you want something visually unlike anywhere else and can give it three or four days.

What are the Five Great Mountains of China?

Taishan in the east, Huashan in the west, Hengshan in the south, another Hengshan (different character, in Shanxi) in the north, and Songshan at the centre. They are a Taoist scheme tied to imperial ritual, not a ranking of scenery — which is why Huangshan, the most beautiful of them all, is not on the list.

Do I need to be fit to climb Chinese mountains?

Not for most of them. Every mountain here has a cable car covering the hard elevation, and the paths above are paved and railed. Taishan's full stairway and Huashan's peak-to-peak circuit are genuine exertion; everything else is a day of walking on steps at your own pace. Knees suffer more than lungs.

Which one has the glass walkways?

Zhangjiajie has both kinds — short glass sections bolted to a cliff on Tianmen Mountain, and a 430-metre free-standing glass bridge in the Grand Canyon an hour away. They get confused constantly. The difference, and which ticket to buy.

Can I combine two of these in one trip?

Yes, if you allow for the distances — these are different provinces, and the pairings that work are the ones sharing a hub. Xi'an gives you Huashan, Chengdu gives you Emeishan, and Zhangjiajie sits on the rail line between Changsha and western Hunan. Trying to string Huangshan and Zhangjiajie together in a week means spending most of it in transit.
