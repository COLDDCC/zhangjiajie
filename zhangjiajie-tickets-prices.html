What changed

The January 2026 price reform

From 1 January 2026 the national forest park abolished its peak and off-peak split and moved to a single year-round entrance price. Most English-language guides still quote the old two-tier structure.

The practical effect: winter visitors no longer get the substantial discount they used to, and summer visitors pay slightly less than the old peak rate. If a page you are reading still says the park costs one price from March to November and a much lower one from December to February, it has not been updated since 2025.

Read every price here as approximate Scenic-area fees in China are adjusted by local authority notice, sometimes at short notice, and booking platforms add service charges and small compulsory insurance amounts on top. Use these figures to build a budget, not to argue at a ticket counter.

Buying

How the ticketing actually works

Bring your passport, every time. Tickets are registered to a named traveller and checked against ID at the gate. Foreign visitors use their passport in place of the Chinese ID card that domestic systems expect, which occasionally means using a staffed counter rather than the automatic readers.

Timed entry slots. Tianmen Mountain and the Grand Canyon assign an entry window when you book. Arriving outside it is a problem; arriving early is not a solution.

Daily capacity caps. In July, August and around public holidays, popular attractions sell out their daily allocation before lunchtime. Book several days ahead in those periods. See which dates to avoid →

Free and reduced entry. Young children generally enter free with ID, and students and older travellers get reductions at most gates. Bring documentation; verbal claims are not accepted.

Changes and refunds. Tianmen Mountain in particular applies escalating change fees as your entry date approaches, and typically permits nothing inside twenty-four hours. Treat a booked mountain date as fixed.

Where the money goes

Trim the lifts, not the gates

The entrance pass covers the eco-buses; the elevator, the three cable cars and the valley tram are each billed on top, roughly as much again as the ticket if you ride everything. If you cut budget, cut the cable cars and take the stairways. They are real walks with their own views.

Questions

Ticket FAQ

How much does Zhangjiajie National Forest Park cost in 2026?

Around ¥240 for an entrance ticket valid four consecutive days, including unlimited use of the eco-buses inside the park. Since 1 January 2026 this is a single year-round price; the old peak and off-peak split no longer applies. Cable cars, the Bailong Elevator and the Ten-Mile Gallery tram are all extra.

Is the Bailong Elevator included in the park ticket?

No. It is charged separately, per direction. You can reach the same plateau at Yuanjiajie by eco-bus and cable car if you would rather not pay for it, though most people ride it at least once for the experience.

Do I need to book Zhangjiajie tickets in advance?

In summer and around Chinese public holidays, yes — several days ahead for Tianmen Mountain and the glass bridge, both of which cap daily entry. In spring and autumn on a weekday, a day ahead is usually enough. The forest park itself rarely sells out.

Can I use one ticket for the forest park, Tianmen Mountain and the glass bridge?

No. They are three separate scenic areas run by different operators, and there is no combined pass. Any product marketed as covering all three is a tour package that has bought the individual tickets on your behalf.

What is the total cost of a three-day Zhangjiajie trip?

For tickets and in-park transport alone, budget roughly ¥450 if you walk everything, ¥750 for a normal mix of lifts, and ¥1,000 or more if you ride every cable car and add the canyon activity combo. Accommodation, food and intercity travel come on top.
